From a research question to software people kept using

Collabberry · 2024 – 2025

How should small, collaborative teams share money fairly when contributions change month to month? I spent a three-month RnDAO fellowship researching that question and published a paper and a model for compensation based on peer-assessed contribution.

  1. Test by hand

    Before writing code, I ran the method, TeamPoints, with Excel and Google Forms. Five to ten organisations used it for monthly compensation cycles for several months.

  2. Fund it

    Secured about $38k in grants and public-goods funding, including an Arbitrum grant and Gitcoin. We paid our own contributors with the Collabberry model, which stretched the runway.

  3. Build it

    Led product decisions and built the backend, smart contracts and infrastructure, working with contributors in frontend, design, product and marketing. The organisations from the manual phase moved onto the software and kept using it after I stepped away.

How I work

Growth plans that the company needs too

A development plan is useful when it connects what someone wants to learn with work the team actually has. I pair people with mentors and give them real responsibility.

Test cheaply before building

A spreadsheet run for months, a one-day prototype, a hackathon bounty. I like to learn what matters before the team commits to building it.

Enough structure to stop guessing

Roadmaps, clear ownership, specs before code. Just enough process that engineers know what matters and why.

Close to the technology

I’ve built supply-chain integrations, a blockchain protocol and distributed systems myself. I still get hands-on when the team needs it, in architecture, code review or a hard bug. Most of my time goes into helping the team do its best work.
